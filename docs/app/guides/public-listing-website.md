Lendiom can generate and host a public website for your organization that lists the land you have for sale. Internally it is called **Unveil**, and in your billing page it is the **Custom Website** add-on.

It is not a site builder. There is no page editor, no theme picker, no drag-and-drop, and no place in the app to write copy. Lendiom renders a fixed template, fills it with your organization details and your available tracts, and serves it. Everything on the page comes from records you already maintain in Lendiom — you keep the site current by keeping your inventory current.

## What the site shows

The site is three pages: the main listing page, a privacy policy, and a terms and conditions page. The main page runs top to bottom in this order.

| Section | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Hero | "Welcome to *your organization name*", a line inviting visitors to browse, a **See properties** button, and a **Lendiom Pay** button that sends buyers to [Lendiom Pay](../../pay/what-is-pay.md) |
| Properties | One card per available tract, or a notice that there is no inventory available |
| How it works | A three-step explanation — contact us, sign paperwork, make the down payment — with your email address and phone number listed as the way to get in touch |
| Privacy | A short paragraph linking to the privacy policy and terms pages |
| Footer | The current year, your organization name, "Site Generated by Lendiom", and links to the privacy and terms pages |

The privacy policy is a stock policy with your organization name and email dropped into it. The terms page is a stock terms document that names your organization, your site's domain, and the state your business is registered in — Lendiom takes that from your legal address, falling back to your billing address if the legal address has no state on it.

There is no contact form. The only contact route offered to a buyer is the email address and phone number in the "How it works" section, so those two fields have to be right. The page's search-engine tag allows indexing but tells crawlers not to follow its links.

## Which tracts appear

A tract shows up on the site when **both** of these are true, and disappears the moment either stops being true:

| Requirement | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| It belongs to a **Land** inventory | Inventories in any other category — residential, multifamily, commercial, other — are skipped entirely, along with everything inside them |
| Its status is **Available** | Under Contract, Pending, Resend Contract, Sold, Late, In Default, and Paid Off are all left off |

A property with no available tracts is dropped from the page completely — its name never appears. If every land property is in that state, the page shows *"We currently have no inventory available! Please check back at a later date."* instead of any cards.

That is the whole filter. There is no per-tract "publish" switch, no draft state, and no way to hold a tract back from the site while leaving it Available. Nothing else about the inventory matters either — a land inventory whose own status is Idea, In Progress, Completed, or Archived still contributes its available tracts.

Properties are listed alphabetically by name, and tracts within a property are ordered by their tract number. See [Working with Tracts](./working-with-tracts.md) for how status and numbering are set, and [Managing Properties](./managing-properties.md) for the inventory record itself.

:::tip
The fastest way to pull a tract off the site is to change its status. Marking it **Under Contract** while paperwork is out keeps it off the public list without deleting anything, and setting it back to **Available** puts it back.
:::

## What each card shows

Each card is titled *property name* — *tract label*, and a tract created within the last 14 days also carries a **NEW** badge that ages off on its own.

Below the title comes the tract's description, then a pricing line, then badges for acreage and state.

The description is the one you wrote on the tract, with all formatting removed. Lendiom stores a plain-text copy of every tract description and it is the plain-text copy that the site uses, so bold text, colors, links, and lists come out as unstyled text. Write descriptions that read well without formatting. A tract with no description shows nothing in that spot.

The pricing line depends on how the tract's **Add Pricing** section is filled in:

| Tract pricing | Line on the site |
| --- | --- |
| Financed | Sales price, down payment, interest rate, monthly payment, and term in years, in one sentence |
| Cash price only | The sales price, followed by an invitation to contact you about owner financing |
| No pricing entered | *"No pricing information, please contact us for more information!"* |

The badges at the bottom of the card show the tract's acreage, and the state from the property's address when one is recorded. Nothing else from the tract shows up on the page: no photos, no map, no coordinates, no parcel numbers, no street address, no cost figures, and no owner information. The tract's latitude and longitude are sent to the site when its address has them recorded, but they are not shown. The template has no image support at all, so there is nowhere to upload listing photos.

:::caution
Whatever you type into a tract's sales price is what a buyer reads. There is no review step, no draft, and no approval between the pricing drawer and the public page. Treat tract pricing on an available tract as published pricing.
:::

## Your address on the web

Every site gets a Lendiom-hosted address of the form `yoursubdomain.lendiom.site`. The subdomain is generated once, from your organization name: spaces come out, the name is lowercased and hyphen-slugged, and the word "company" is stripped. "Demo Land Company" becomes `demoland`, and the site answers at `demoland.lendiom.site`. Nothing in the app renames it.

You can also point your own domain at the site. That is set up on Lendiom's side, and once it is recorded, Lendiom raises a DNS job for it:

| Your domain | What you point it at |
| --- | --- |
| An apex domain, such as `demolandcompany.com` | A records supplied by Lendiom |
| A subdomain, such as `land.demolandcompany.com` | A CNAME record supplied by Lendiom |

Lendiom then verifies the domain resolves correctly. Your organization's owner — or an admin, if there is no owner — gets an email either way. Success brings a *Custom Domain Verified* email with a link to your site. Failure brings a *Custom Domain Verification Failed* email naming the error and asking you to check the DNS records, confirm the target, allow up to 48 hours for propagation, and make sure the domain is not already in use elsewhere.

Until a custom domain is verified, the site keeps answering on its `.lendiom.site` address, and that address stays live afterward too.

## Turning it on

The site is not self-serve. In **Org Settings → Billing**, the Addons card lists **Custom Website** with an Enable button that is permanently disabled — see [Add-Ons](../billing/add-ons.md) and [Settings Your Lendiom Contact Controls](../how-it-works/support-controlled-settings.md) for the other settings that work this way. Email [Lendiom Support](mailto:support@lendiom.com) to have a site created, to change your subdomain, to add a custom domain, or to take a site down. None of those four actions exist as a button anywhere in the app.

There is one exception. During [PayArc onboarding](../payment-processing/onboarding-payarc.md), the **Support** step asks for your business website, because the payment processor requires one. If you do not have a website, that step offers a **Generate!** button which creates the site immediately and fills the field with the resulting `.lendiom.site` address. After that, the button turns into **Visit**. The step is part of a one-time wizard, so once your support information is on file the wizard does not return to it — which leaves support as the route for everything afterward.

Both site requests — reading your site and generating one — require the **billing \*** permission on your role, the same "all" action needed to change add-ons. Read-only billing access is not enough. See [Roles and Permissions](../security/roles-and-permissions.md).

## Keeping it accurate

A short list of what actually keeps the public page right:

- Set tract status the day it changes. Status is the only thing standing between a sold tract and a buyer's phone call.
- Fill in pricing on tracts you want priced publicly, and leave it empty on the ones you would rather discuss.
- Keep your organization email and phone number current under [your business settings](./setting-up-your-business.md). If you run the communication portal, the site publishes that number instead of your main one.
- Record a state on each land property's address if you want the state badge to appear on its cards.
- Write tract descriptions as plain sentences, since formatting is stripped.
