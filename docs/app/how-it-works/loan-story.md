The **Story** tab answers one question about a loan: what is going on with it, and how did it get that way? Instead of reading the schedule, the transaction table, the notes, and the conversation history separately and holding the picture in your head, you get all of them assembled onto one page.

Open any loan and the tab sits in the header row, labeled **Story** with a purple **Beta** tag. Loans only — rentals do not have it.

![The loan page tab row with Overview, Story (tagged Beta) and Details](/img/docs/app/how-it-works/loan-story/01.png)

## What the page is made of

The tab always opens with a blue **Story is in Beta** banner, then a narrative card, then three sections that start collapsed:

| Section | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| Narrative card | The headline, the loan's [status](./loan-status.md) tag, and a plain-English write-up |
| Active issues | One colored alert per current problem |
| Balance breakdown | A table of every outstanding line item, with a total |
| Timeline | Up to 50 dated events, oldest at the top |

Click a section header to expand it. The tab is read-only; nothing on it changes data.

![The Story tab, with the beta banner, the narrative card, and the collapsed section headers below it](/img/docs/app/how-it-works/loan-story/02.png)

## The narrative card

The card's title is a generated headline plus the loan's status tag. The headline is one of a few fixed shapes:

| Situation | Headline |
| --- | --- |
| Nothing outstanding | `Paid off`, `Current and in good standing`, or — for any other status — the raw status code, not its friendly label: `in-default`, `repo`, `pending-payoff` |
| Past-due installments outstanding | `$1,240.00 owed across 3 component(s)` |
| Fees only outstanding | `$85.00 owed` |

Below the title is the narrative itself, and this is where the two modes matter.

### Polished vs. raw facts

The card has a **Polished** switch. With it on, you get a two-to-four paragraph write-up in prose. With it off — or when no polished version exists — you get a one-line fallback built from the headline and a count of active issues, such as *"$1,240.00 owed across 3 component(s). 2 active issue(s); see breakdown below."*

Opening the tab fires two requests: the first skips the polish so the page paints immediately, the second fetches the polished narrative and swaps it in. While that second request runs you see *Generating polished narrative…* and a placeholder.

The **Refresh** button discards the stored narrative and generates a new one on the spot. Next to it, *Polished 3 hours ago* tells you when the current one was written; hover it for the exact timestamp.

![The Story tab narrative card, with the headline, the prose summary and the Polished toggle](/img/docs/app/how-it-works/loan-story/03.png)

### Yes, a language model writes the prose

Being direct about this: **the polished narrative is generated by a large language model.** Lendiom sends the assembled facts to OpenAI and asks for a rewrite. Everything else on the page — headline, balance table, active issues, timeline — is computed from your data with no model involved.

What gets sent is the headline, the status, every balance component, every active issue, and the **last 20 timeline events**, including the text of any notes and SMS messages in that window and the names of the staff attached to those events. If that is more than you want leaving your account, do not open the tab.

The prompt instructs the model to use only the facts it was given, preserve amounts and dates exactly, and add no advice or speculation about cause. That is an instruction, not a guarantee.

:::caution
Treat the polished paragraphs as a summary, not as a record. Before you repeat any figure to a buyer or put it in writing, flip the **Polished** switch off and confirm it against the balance breakdown and the timeline underneath. The banner at the top of the tab says the same thing.
:::

One narrative is stored per loan and reused until the underlying facts change, which is why a loan can show the same wording for weeks and then rewrite itself the day a payment lands.

:::info
The polished narrative depends on a service Lendiom configures on the server side. If it is unavailable, the request fails quietly: the tab still renders everything else, the **Polished** switch stays disabled, and pressing **Refresh** produces no visible change and no error message.
:::

## Active issues

Only four kinds of problem are ever raised here:

| Issue | When it appears |
| --- | --- |
| Past-due installments | One or more scheduled payments due today or earlier are not fully paid. Red |
| Setup fee unpaid | A setup fee on the loan was created and never paid. Orange |
| Other fees outstanding | The loan's other-fees bucket is above zero. Orange |
| Auto-pay is not enabled | The loan permits auto-draft but it is not currently active. Orange |

The auto-pay alert tries to tell you *why* it is off, pulling the reason from the most recent time it was disabled, and falls back to a generic description when none was recorded. Late fees and property tax show up in the balance breakdown but never raise an alert here.

## Balance breakdown

The table lists one row per outstanding item, with **Item**, **Due**, **Paid**, **Remaining**, and **Reason** columns, and a **Total** row at the bottom.

| Row kind | What produces it |
| --- | --- |
| Installment | Each schedule payment due on or before today that is not fully paid. Labeled `Installment 4 (due Mar 1, 2026)`, with a reason of *Not yet paid*, *Payment past due*, or *Underpaid by $120.00* |
| Late fees | The loan's [late fee](./late-fees.md) balance, when above zero |
| Other fees | The loan's [other fees](./loan-other-fees.md) balance, when above zero |
| Property tax | The [property tax](../guides/property-taxes.md) balance, when above zero |
| Setup fee | Each unpaid setup fee, with the reason *Created but never paid* |

:::caution
The **Total** here is what is currently owed, not the loan's payoff amount. Installments not yet due are excluded on purpose, and so is remaining principal. For a payoff figure, use [Recording a Loan Payoff](../guides/recording-a-loan-payoff.md).
:::

Installment labels and their due dates are rendered in your organization's timezone, falling back to Central if none is set.

## Timeline

Events run oldest to newest with a colored icon per entry, the event title, the date, a detail line, and an attribution line reading *by Jane Doe* or *by Lendiom*. Anything the system did on its own — a nightly late fee, an auto-drafted payment — is attributed to Lendiom.

| Event | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Payment received / Late payment / Partial payment | Payment, principal, and down payment transactions. Lateness and shortfall are read off the schedule rows the payment covered |
| Payment reversed | A [reversed](../guides/reversing-a-transaction.md) transaction, with the reason and any reversal note |
| Late fee assessed / waived / reversed | Late fee transactions. See [Waiving Late Fees](../guides/waiving-late-fees.md) |
| Other fee added | A charge that increases the other-fees balance |
| Setup fee created / paid | The loan's setup fees |
| Auto-pay enabled / disabled | The loan's auto-draft history. See [Automatic Payments](./automatic-payments.md) |
| Note added / System note | Notes on the loan, both yours and the system's |
| SMS sent / received | Human-written messages with the client, from the [Conversations inbox](../communication/conversations-inbox.md) |
| Call placed / received | Calls with the client — direction, status, and duration only |

The list is capped at **50 events**. Past that, the oldest are dropped and the newest 50 are kept.

Some things are filtered out deliberately. Failed transactions never appear. [Automated messages](../communication/automated-messages.md) — reminders, late notices, anything carrying the standard opt-out line — are stripped so real conversations are not buried. Setup fee payments appear once, from the fee itself, not again from the transaction.

:::info
Messages and calls are attached to the client, not to the loan. If one client holds several loans with you, the same texts and calls appear on the Story tab of every one of them.
:::

:::caution
Timeline entries are not clickable. They tell you a transaction exists but will not take you to it; scroll down to the transaction list to open it. Setup fee payments also carry no date and render as *No date value set* at the very top of the list.
:::

## How this differs from the Timeline and the transaction list

Three things on a loan look similar and are not:

| | Story timeline | Timeline feature | Transaction list |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who writes it | Lendiom, from existing records | You, by hand | Lendiom, when money moves |
| Where it lives | The Story tab on a loan | Clients and tracts | Below the tabs on every loan |
| Contains | Payments, fees, auto-pay changes, notes, texts, calls | Whatever you type into it | Transactions only |
| Editable | No | Yes — add entries | Yes — reverse, waive, revise dates |
| Complete | No, capped at 50 events | Yes | Yes |

The transaction list stays below the tabs whichever tab you are on, so you can read the story and check a transaction without navigating away. When the two disagree, the transaction list wins — it is the record, and the story is a reading of it.

## What is not on this tab

- Escrow activity, interest accrual, and remaining principal
- Future installments that are not yet due
- Documents, files, signature requests, and [unapplied payments](./unapplied-payments.md)
- Buttons to fix anything — use the [Action Center](./loan-action-center.md) or [add a transaction](../guides/adding-a-transaction.md)
- Anything older than the 50 most recent events

## Who can see it

Anyone whose role holds `loan` **read** — the same permission that opens the loan at all. There is no separate permission for the Story tab.

:::warning
That means the Story tab shows note text and SMS content to people whose roles do not include `loan::notes` or communication access. If a role is deliberately kept away from that material, review it against [Roles and Permissions](../security/roles-and-permissions.md).
:::
