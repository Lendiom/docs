The home screen is the first thing you see after you sign in. It answers one question up front: how much do you owe right now? Everything else on the page explains where that number comes from.

![The Lendiom Pay home screen at phone width, with the Balance Due header above the accounts list](/img/docs/pay/home-screen/01.png)

## The Balance Due at the top

The large amount at the top of the screen, under the words **Balance Due**, is everything you owe across your whole account added together. It is made up of three things:

- **Your loans.** For each loan, the amount owed is the payments you have not made yet, plus any late fees, plus any other fees, plus unpaid interest carried over from earlier payments.
- **Your rentals.** The rent owed on each rental listed on this screen. Late fees on a rental are counted in the rental's own row but not in this total.
- **Your unpaid invoices.** The remaining balance on any invoice that has not been paid in full.

The same total appears again in the menu, so you can see it from any page.

:::info
Each loan, rental, and invoice also shows its own amount on the right side of its row. Those individual amounts are the ones to look at when you want to pay a specific item. The number at the top is only a sum.

Rentals have one quirk worth knowing about. A rental's own row includes its late fees, but the Balance Due at the top counts only the rent owed on that rental, without the late fees. So if you have late fees on a rental, that row shows more than the amount it added to the top number, and the rows will not add up to the total. Loan and invoice rows match what they add to the total exactly.
:::

## What the total leaves out

Two things are deliberately kept out of the total, and both surprise people.

**Property tax is its own bucket.** If any of your loans has property tax due, a separate **Property Tax Due** card appears with its own amount, and the loan's row says "Property Tax Due" underneath its name. That money is real and it is owed, but it is not folded into the Balance Due at the top, and paying your regular payment does not pay it. It is paid on its own. See [Paying Property Taxes](./guides/paying-property-taxes.md).

**Refinanced loans are shown but not counted.** If one of your loans was refinanced into a new one, the old loan stays visible in your list with a status of "Refinanced" so you can still look at its history. It is not added to the Balance Due, it is not used for your next due date, and it is not used for your last payment date. No further payments can be made on it. The new loan is the one that counts.

![The Balance Due header and the feature cards beneath it at phone width](/img/docs/pay/home-screen/01.png)

## Why some loans do not appear at all

Your list shows the agreements that are still live. A loan disappears from the screen entirely once it is paid off, marked inactive, canceled, repossessed, or still a draft that was never finalized. Rentals disappear once they are terminated, once eviction is complete, or while they are still a draft.

If a loan you expected is missing, that usually means it reached one of those states. Nothing was deleted, and your payment history is not lost. Contact the company you pay and ask them to confirm the status.

:::caution
If a loan you are still paying on has vanished from this screen, do not stop paying. Contact the company you buy or rent from right away. Lendiom Support cannot see or change your account status.
:::

## Next Due Date and Last Payment Date

Two cards sit under the balance.

**Next Due Date** is the soonest date across your loans and your unpaid invoices.

There is one important exception. When a loan is marked **Late**, the date shown is not a future date. It is the due date you already passed, the payment that was missed. That is intentional, because that date is the one that needs your attention. If the date on this card is in the past, it means a payment is overdue, not that the screen is stale.

**Last Payment Date** is the most recent payment received across the loans counted in your balance.

If either card shows a dash (`-`), there is no date to show yet. On a brand new account, that is normal.

![The Lendiom Pay home screen showing a loan row whose status reads Late](/img/docs/pay/home-screen/03.png)

## The balance breakdown on daily-interest loans

Some loans charge interest that builds up every day rather than in fixed chunks. On those loans, you can tap the **Balance Due** amount on the loan's own page to open a breakdown showing:

- Payments Due
- Unpaid Interest Due
- Late Fees Due
- Other Fees Due
- Total Due

There is one thing this breakdown does not include, and it matters. **Interest that has built up since your last payment is not in the Total Due.** That amount is recalculated overnight and is tracked separately. So on a daily-interest loan, the figure you see is what is currently owed as of the last time the account settled up. It is not a payoff quote.

:::tip
If you want the amount that would close out the loan completely, look for the **Pay Off Balance** figure on the loan's page. It is calculated for a specific date and shows that date next to it. That number, not the Balance Due, is the one to use when you are trying to pay a loan off.
:::

![The loan balance due breakdown at phone width, itemising payments due, unpaid interest, late fees and other fees above the total](/img/docs/pay/home-screen/06.png)

## Reading the lists

Below the cards, your items are grouped into **Invoices**, **Rentals**, and **Loans**. When you have an invoice with money owed on it, invoices move to the top of the page so you see them first. When nothing is due on them, they drop to the bottom.

Each row shows the name, the current status, and the amount owed. An amount shown in red means money is currently owed on that item. Tap **View Details** on any row to open it, see its transaction history, and make a payment.

Some rows carry an extra warning next to the status, such as **Down Payment Due** or a setup fee that has not been paid. Those are amounts that have to be paid separately from your regular payment. Matching alerts also appear near the top of the screen so they are hard to miss.

The **Rentals** and **Invoices** sections only appear when you have something in them. If you have no rentals, no Rentals section appears.

**Loans** works differently. That section is always on the screen, even when you have none. If you have no loans, it simply reads "You currently have no loans." Seeing that message does not mean anything is wrong with your account.

## Switching between English and Spanish

Lendiom Pay is fully available in English and Spanish.

Tap the menu button in the top-left corner to open the menu. Near the bottom of the list you will see **Spanish** (or **English**, if you are already reading Spanish). Tap it and the wording across the portal switches immediately.

![The Lendiom Pay side menu open at phone width, showing the account name and the menu entries](/img/docs/pay/home-screen/05.png)

Two things worth knowing about that switch:

- **The choice is saved in the browser you are using.** If you sign in from a different phone or computer, or clear your browsing data, you will need to pick your language again there.
- **Text messages follow a different setting.** The sign-in codes and account-number texts you receive use the language recorded on your account by the company you pay. Changing the language here does not change those messages. If your texts are arriving in the wrong language, ask that company to update the language on your record.

## If the screen will not load

If something goes wrong loading your information, you will see a red box reading "We were unable to load this page" with a **Try Again** button. Tap it. A weak connection is the most common cause, and a second attempt usually works.

If you see a message about a newer version being available, tap **Reload**. Your browser is holding on to an older copy of the app.

If **Try Again** keeps failing, close the tab, reopen [pay.lendiom.com](https://pay.lendiom.com), and sign in again. See [How do I log into Lendiom Pay?](./logging-in.md) if you need your sign-in steps.
